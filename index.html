Edge and frequency by true count

How it is calculated, and assumptions
  • Edge per count. For each true-count bucket the shoe composition is rebuilt from the true count and the average decks remaining in that bucket. Every player two-card hand against every dealer upcard is then enumerated, with cards removed from the shoe. The play of each hand (hit, stand, double, split, surrender) is solved exactly, including resplits and the blackjack peek.
  • Frequency per count. A seeded 300,000-round shoe simulation of Hi-Lo counting with your deck count, penetration and number of other players gives how often each true count occurs at the start of a round. Other players use a simple hit/stand rule just to consume cards.
  • Bet limits. 1 unit is the table minimum; every bet is kept between it and the table maximum. In the default spread a count has “an edge” when its player edge (including insurance profit, if you selected it) is above zero.
  • Combining. Overall edge = Σ(frequency × bet × edge) / Σ(frequency × bet). Profit per round is in bet units; a bet of 0 is a sit-out, which is counted as a round dealt but not played.
  • Strategy. “Basic + count indices” assumes you play optimally for the current true count, which is what a perfect index-play deviation set achieves. “Basic strategy only” uses the fresh-shoe strategy at every count.
  • Insurance. “Basic + insurance” is decided when insurance is offered, i.e. after the round's cards are out: your two cards, every other player's cards and the dealer's ace all update the count before you decide, while your bet was sized on the count at the start of the round. Insurance (a half-unit bet paying 2:1) is taken when that updated count is at or above the threshold you set (default +3): the Hi-Lo true count using the decks left at that moment, or the running count for KO / unbalanced. Its value comes from the simulated shoes: 0.5 × (3 × P(hole card is a ten) − 1) from the cards actually unseen. So a starting bucket can be insured on only some dealer aces (◐). The index-play option does not take insurance.
  • Unbalanced counts (KO, and the single-deck A0 system). The count is not divided by decks remaining; bets are keyed to the raw running count since the shuffle. KO tags 2–7 +1, 8–9 0, T–A −1 and starts at −4 × (decks − 1), so it ends near +4 at the bottom of the shoe on average. With these counts the rows are the actual running count (≤ −1 and ≥ +10 are open-ended, so at 6–8 decks the top row covers a wide range of strongly positive counts). Each bucket's shoe is rebuilt from its average cards dealt and running count, treating the count's excess over its natural drift as extra low cards seen in place of high cards.
  • Multiple boxes. Each box gets the bet in the table, so the edge per unit wagered is the same as with one box, while expected profit per round is multiplied by the number of boxes. Your boxes use cards from the shoe, so they shift the count distribution (and, with auto shuffle, count toward the players at the table). Boxes share one dealer hand, so their results are correlated (measured correlation about 0.37); that is included in the standard deviation and N0. Rounds per hour is left as you enter it, so lower it when you play more boxes.
  • Approximations. After the first three cards, later draws use the remaining composition as fixed probabilities (negligible error for 2+ decks; the engine agrees with real-shoe simulation to about ±0.03% in tests, and with published multi-deck house edges). Per-round variance is taken as 1.32 per unit bet, and the count is the standard Hi-Lo.
  • Not modelled: insurance / side bets, no-hole-card (European) games, cut-card effects, bankroll-dependent bet sizing, and the small extra cards other players' hits remove.
